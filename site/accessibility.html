Accessibility Statement

What we have built into this site for assistive technology, where we fall short, and how to tell us.

Home  /  Accessibility
Back to Home

We want this site to work for everyone, including guests using a screen reader, keyboard only, magnification, or a device set to reduce motion. This page records what we have built in, and how to tell us when something does not work.

What we have done

  • Keyboard. Every control — navigation, menu category tabs, the FAQ, the footer sections — is reachable and operable by keyboard. Menu tabs also respond to arrow, Home and End keys.
  • Visible focus. A gold focus ring is drawn on whatever you have tabbed to, and it is never removed.
  • Skip link. A “Skip to main content” link is the first thing you reach on every page.
  • Real semantics. Headings are in order, the menu tabs are a proper tab list, the FAQ uses native <details> so it works with JavaScript off, and the footer section headings are real buttons with their state announced.
  • Images. Every photograph has descriptive alternative text; decorative layers are hidden from assistive technology.
  • Contrast. Body and heading colours were chosen to meet WCAG 2.1 AA on both our light and dark backgrounds — gold text is a darker gold on the light ground and a lighter gold on the dark hero for exactly this reason.
  • Touch targets. Interactive controls on phones are at least 42×42 pixels, and most are 44 or larger — comfortably above the 24×24 minimum WCAG asks for.
  • Text scaling. Layout is built in relative units, so increasing your browser or system text size reflows the page instead of clipping it.

If you have set a preference on your device

This site listens for three system settings and adapts automatically:

  • Reduce motion — the drifting background stops, panels stop sliding, and hover movement is switched off. You still get the feedback, just without the movement.
  • Reduce transparency — the frosted-glass panels become solid, so text never sits over a busy background.
  • Increase contrast — backgrounds go solid, borders become gold, and body text moves to full black or white.

Where we know we fall short

We would rather say this plainly than claim more than we have earned:

  • Our gallery photographs have short alternative text that names the dish; it does not describe the plating in detail.
  • The site has not yet been audited by an external accessibility specialist, and it has not been tested with every screen reader and browser combination.
  • Content on services we link to — online ordering, maps, social platforms — is outside our control.

Standard we aim at

We aim to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA. This is a self-assessment, not a certified audit.

In the restaurant

If it would help to know about access at the restaurant itself before you visit — entry, seating, or anything else — please call and ask and we will describe it honestly.
